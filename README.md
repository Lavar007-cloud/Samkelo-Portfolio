## 1. Agile Team Charter

### Evidence

[View the Agile Team Charter](evidence/Team_Charter.docx)

### Reflection

**Situation**

Our team developed a Team Charter for the DealNest Discount Discovery App, which aims to help students and budget-conscious shoppers in Cape Town find retail discounts in one place. The charter established the project's objectives, team roles, responsibilities, communication methods, values and milestones to guide us throughout the project.

**Task**

My assigned role was Backend Developer / Writer. My responsibilities included backend API development, database design and implementation, server infrastructure setup, API documentation and technical writing. I needed to understand these responsibilities and how they contributed to the team's overall objectives. It was also important to understand the team's expectations regarding communication, collaboration and completing tasks.

**Action**

I reviewed the Team Charter to understand the purpose and vision of DealNest, my assigned responsibilities and the expectations agreed upon by the team. I familiarised myself with the communication plan, including team meetings, progress updates and the use of GitHub for technical discussions. I also reviewed the team's core values, such as collaboration, transparency, quality and continuous improvement, as well as the project goals and milestones. This helped me understand how my backend development and documentation responsibilities fitted into the overall project and why it was important to work together towards our shared goals.

**Result**

The Team Charter gave me a clearer understanding of my responsibilities and the standards expected from each team member. It showed me the importance of having clear roles, effective communication and shared goals when working on a group project. I also learned that a team charter can help keep members accountable and provide direction throughout the development process. This experience helped me appreciate the value of planning and collaboration in Agile software development.

## 2. User Stories and Tasks

### Evidence

[View the DealNest User Stories and Tasks](evidence/DealNest_User_Stories.docx)

### Reflection

**Situation**

During the development of the DealNest Discount Discovery App, our team identified different features needed to help customers discover discounts, save their favourite deals, compare offers and claim vouchers. We documented these requirements as user stories for customers, DealNest Plus customers, store owners, administrators and all users. These stories helped describe what each type of user needed from the application.

**Task**

As the Backend Developer / Writer, my role was to understand the application requirements and how they related to backend development, database design, API development and technical documentation. I needed to understand the functionality expected by different users so that the backend could support the features planned for the application.

**Action**

I reviewed the user stories to understand the requirements of the different user roles and the functionality they expected from DealNest. I looked at features such as browsing deals, saving favourites, generating voucher codes, searching and filtering promotions, and allowing store owners to publish and manage promotions. I considered how these features could relate to backend services, database records and API functionality. Reviewing the stories helped me understand how user requirements can be broken down into smaller features and tasks that guide the software development process.

**Result**

The user stories gave me a clearer understanding of the features DealNest was intended to provide and the needs of its different users. I learned that user stories help a development team understand the purpose of each feature before implementation begins. I also gained a better understanding of how user requirements guide backend development, database planning and technical documentation. This showed me the importance of understanding the user's needs when designing and developing software.

## 4. Sprint Backlog

### Evidence

[View the DealNest Sprint Backlog](<evidence/Sprint Backlog.png>)

### Reflection

**Situation**

During the development of the DealNest Discount Discovery App, our team organised the project work into multiple sprints. The Sprint Backlog included tasks relating to authentication, customer functionality, business management, administrator features, maps, testing, and performance optimisation. As a Backend Developer and Writer, my assigned tasks included building the `POST /api/auth/refresh-token` endpoint and gathering functional requirements for the Administrator role.

**Task**

My responsibility was to understand my assigned tasks and how they contributed to the application's backend functionality and security. This involved understanding the purpose of the refresh-token endpoint and identifying the functions and permissions required for the Administrator role. I also needed to understand how my responsibilities fitted into the team's sprint planning and overall project objectives.

**Action**

I reviewed the Sprint Backlog to identify my assigned tasks and understand how they related to the wider development process. I considered the role of refresh-token functionality in maintaining user authentication and the importance of defining Administrator requirements to support appropriate access control. I also examined how the team's other tasks, such as registration, login, role-based authorisation, and administrator user management, related to the application's security. This helped me understand the dependencies between backend functionality and other parts of the system.

**Result**

Reviewing the Sprint Backlog helped me understand how a large project is divided into smaller, manageable tasks and distributed across team members and sprints. I gained a clearer understanding of my responsibilities and how authentication, authorisation, and requirements gathering contribute to DealNest. I also learned the importance of coordinating backend tasks with related features to support the development of a secure and functional application.
