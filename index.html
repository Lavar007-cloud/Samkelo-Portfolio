Agile project learning

Agile STAR reflections

These reflections describe my understanding of the DealNest project artefacts and my assigned responsibilities. They focus on planning, requirements and learning rather than claiming tasks were implemented when they were not.

01 · Team charter

Understanding team roles

Situation: Our team developed a Team Charter for the DealNest Discount Discovery App, which aims to help students and budget-conscious shoppers in Cape Town find retail discounts in one place. The charter established project objectives, roles, responsibilities, communication methods, values and milestones.

Task: My assigned role was Backend Developer / Writer, with responsibilities relating to backend APIs, database design, server setup, API documentation and technical writing. I needed to understand these responsibilities and the team's expectations.

Action: I reviewed the Team Charter to understand the project vision, my assigned responsibilities, the communication plan, team meetings, progress updates and use of GitHub for technical discussions. I also considered the team's values, goals and milestones.

Result: The charter clarified my responsibilities and showed me why clear roles, communication, accountability and shared goals are important in Agile teamwork.

02 · User stories

Understanding user requirements

Situation: The team identified features for customers, DealNest Plus customers, store owners, administrators and all users. These requirements were documented as user stories.

Task: I needed to understand how user requirements related to backend development, database design, API functionality and documentation.

Action: I reviewed stories involving browsing deals, saving favourites, generating voucher codes, searching and filtering promotions, and allowing store owners to publish and manage promotions. I considered how these features might connect to backend services, database records and APIs.

Result: I learned how user stories explain the purpose of a feature and help the team break requirements into smaller tasks before implementation.

03 · Product backlog

Understanding assigned tasks

Situation: The team used a Product Backlog to organise the work required for DealNest. My assigned tasks included an authentication endpoint and gathering functional requirements for the Administrator role.

Task: I was assigned the task of developing the POST /api/auth/refresh-token endpoint and gathering requirements to clarify what the Administrator should be able to do.

Action: I identified the purpose of each task. Refresh-token functionality relates to authentication and security, while Administrator requirements help clarify the functions and permissions needed for the Admin role.

Result: I gained a clearer understanding of how authentication, security and requirements gathering contribute to the project, and why a backlog helps the team plan and track work.

04 · Sprint backlog

Planning work across sprints

Situation: The team organised development into sprints covering authentication, customer functionality, business management, administrator features, maps, testing and performance optimisation.

Task: I needed to understand how my assigned refresh-token endpoint and Administrator requirements fitted into sprint planning and the application's security.

Action: I reviewed the Sprint Backlog and considered how refresh-token functionality supports authentication, while Administrator requirements support access control. I also examined how registration, login, role-based authorisation and administrator user management relate to security.

Result: I learned how larger projects are divided into manageable tasks and how backend responsibilities depend on related features across a team.

05 · Agile logbook

Reflecting on backend requirements

Situation: My DealNest responsibilities involved the refresh-token endpoint and gathering requirements for the Administrator role.

Task: I needed to understand the expected endpoint behaviour and the functions and permissions required for the Administrator role, including potential errors and security concerns.

Action: I focused on how refresh tokens support authentication and considered valid, invalid and expired tokens. I also considered access to administrative functions, the need to clarify requirements, and API responses and error handling before implementation.

Result: I improved my understanding of backend development, authentication, access control and requirements gathering, and learned the importance of planning and identifying issues before implementation.

Learning outcome

What I take forward

Across these Agile activities, I learned to connect user needs, project planning and backend responsibilities. I also developed a better appreciation for communicating clearly, understanding requirements before implementation, and coordinating tasks with the wider team.
